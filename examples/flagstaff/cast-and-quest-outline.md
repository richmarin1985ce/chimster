# Flagstaff — original cast and political conflict (provisional)
All named characters here are original proposals, not existing Fallout NPCs. Names and functions can change. Do not equate a faction's public claims with the story's moral verdict.
## The central conflict: the capital without its ruler
With Caesar campaigning west, the provincial capital's administrators face wartime demands for supplies, soldiers and captives. Merchant wealth, military authority and the civilian economy are mutually dependent. The Courier's abduction introduces a larger question: who owns the road, and what happens when the army needs more than the city can provide?
## Candidate cast
| NPC | Role and dramatic tension |
|---|---|
| Vesta Marrow | Affluent caravan-house proprietor; believes predictable law makes commerce possible, but the house's profits implicate her in captivity. |
| Silvan Rusk | Bighorner rancher whose estate supports extended families and enslaved workers; faces a requisition that could destroy his business. |
| Cassian Vale | Original Legion quartermaster, loyal to the war effort; forced to choose between obeying orders and keeping the capital supplied. |
| Livia Sable | Civilian ledger clerk who understands the paper trail behind private slaving contracts; wants security, not heroism. |
| Ash | Captive pit worker and possible companion; distrusts both merchants and Legion authorities; personal goal is to locate separated family. |
| Nell Fallow | Roadside repair-shop owner and keeper of pre-War motoring memorabilia; treats the Mother Road as a cultural inheritance rather than military property. |
| Morrow Finch | Disguised surveyor working with an original cell claiming Chicago Enclave ties; research and reconnaissance rather than an overt occupying army. |
| Tallow | Former caravan guard who guides fugitives through old service routes; pragmatic and unreliable. |
## Faction structure
Independent chartered caravan houses (provisional institution), ranchers' informal association, Legion supply administration, captive solidarity networks and independent roadside communities. Neither merchant affluence nor Legion road safety implies emancipation or equality. The Enclave reconnaissance cell is hidden, fragmentary and internally divided; avoid making it the main antagonist or deciding Chicago canon.
## Main quest scaffold
CR_MQ01 The Roman Holiday — kidnapping, confiscation receipt, first decisions in the slave pit.
CR_MQ02 Don't Fence Me In — escape through multiple play styles, establish cover or status, recover gear and determine whether others escape.
CR_MQ03 All Roads Lead to Rome — work or bargain with competing businesses, trace a chain of obligations.
CR_MQ04 The Best Things in Life Are Free — contend with provisioning orders, regional shortages and legitimacy of local rule.
CR_MQ05 Long Distance Call — investigate cryptic eastbound road surveys and discover the optional intelligence subplot.
CR_MQ06 The Price of Civilization — player-driven resolution of capital politics and road access; consequences based on prior actions.
CR_MQ07 East of the Sun — return to Mojave when ready, independent conditional slides and optional endgame interoperability.
Side quests should allow solutions by Speech, Barter, Survival, Science, Repair, Medicine, Sneak and combat where appropriate, with some fail-forward outcomes. Major endings should consider captivity and liberation, commerce and coercion, military supply, and survival of individual communities. Do not overwrite vanilla faction endings. Optional cross-plugin hooks require explicit compatibility testing.
